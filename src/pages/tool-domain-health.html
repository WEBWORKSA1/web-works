What we check and why it matters

Email authentication. Since February 2024 Gmail and Yahoo require bulk senders to authenticate with SPF, DKIM and DMARC, and every sender gets better inbox placement with them. A domain without DMARC can be spoofed in phishing emails that carry your name.

Parked domains. If a domain never sends email, the safest setup is an SPF record of v=spf1 -all plus a DMARC policy of p=reject. Attackers prefer unprotected, unused domains.

DNSSEC. Signing your zone lets resolvers reject forged answers. It must be enabled at your DNS host and confirmed with a DS record at the registry.

CAA. A one-line record that limits which certificate authorities may issue certificates for your name.

DKIM selectors. DKIM keys sit under a selector name chosen by your email provider, and DNS cannot list them. We try the selectors used by Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Mailchimp, Zoho and other common services. “Not found” means none of those, not necessarily no DKIM.
