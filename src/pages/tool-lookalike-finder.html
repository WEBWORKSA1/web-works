Lookalike domain finder

Builds the typos and look-alikes a phisher would register for your domain, then checks live DNS to show which ones already exist and which can receive email.

Include:
Enter your domain to scan up to 300 variants. It takes about a minute and runs entirely in your browser.

How attackers pick lookalikes

  • Typos: a missing, doubled or swapped letter, or a neighbouring key on the keyboard (exampel.com, exanple.com).
  • Look-alike letters: rn for m, vv for w, 0 for o, 1 for l. These fool people reading quickly on a phone.
  • Added words: login, secure, support, pay or app attached to your name. Common in fake invoice and password-reset emails.
  • Other extensions: your name on .co, .net or a cheap new extension.

Reading the results

A variant marked registered exists in DNS. That alone isn’t proof of bad intent: many are parked or owned defensively, sometimes by you. The flag to act on is accepts email: a lookalike with MX records can send and receive mail that pretends to be you.

What to do about one

  1. Look it up to find the registrar and its abuse contact.
  2. If it hosts phishing or malware, report it to the registrar and to Google Safe Browsing.
  3. If it uses your trademark in bad faith, a UDRP complaint can transfer it to you.
  4. Register the most dangerous unregistered variants yourself. A handful of defensive names costs less than one incident.
