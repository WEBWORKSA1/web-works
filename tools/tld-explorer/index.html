About the root zone

The root zone is the top of the domain name system. It lists every top-level domain and the servers responsible for it, and IANA publishes it as a plain list that changes whenever an extension is added or retired. This page reads that list, plus IANA’s root zone database for each extension’s type and operator, on every refresh. Changes between refreshes are logged in the daily brief.

Types of extension

  • Generic extensions such as .com, .works or .shop are open to anyone or to a defined community.
  • Country-code extensions are two letters (.ca, .in, .uk) and follow each country’s own rules; some require local presence.
  • Internationalized extensions use non-Latin scripts and appear in DNS as an xn-- Punycode form.
  • Sponsored and restricted extensions limit who can register, such as .gov or .museum.
  • Brand extensions belong to one company for its own use, such as a car maker or a bank. Nobody else can register names under them.

RDAP support matters because it decides whether the domain lookup can read registration dates directly. Most generic extensions publish RDAP; many country codes do not yet.
