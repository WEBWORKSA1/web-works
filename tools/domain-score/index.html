Domain name score

A 0–100 rating built from six factors that buyers and brokers weigh. Every point is explained, so you can argue with it.

Compare:
Enter a domain to see its score and breakdown.

How the score is built

FactorPointsWhat earns them
Length25Shorter names are easier to remember and type. Full marks at four letters or fewer.
Extension20.com earns the most because it’s the default people type. Strong country codes and .io, .ai and .co follow.
Clarity20One real word beats two, two beat three. Invented words score on how cleanly they read.
Pronunciation15A healthy mix of vowels and consonants, no long consonant clusters, no tripled letters.
Characters10Letters only. Hyphens and digits cost points, except for short all-number names, which have their own market.
Commercial pull10Words from industries that pay for traffic and leads, such as finance, insurance, health, travel and software.

A score is not a price

What a domain sells for depends on a buyer who needs that exact name, and on comparable sales. Use the score to rank a shortlist or to sanity-check an asking price, then read How to value a domain name for the comparable-sales method.
