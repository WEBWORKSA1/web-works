Domain & website glossary

70 terms from registries, DNS, the domain aftermarket and website building, explained in plain words.

A B C D E G H I L M N P R S T U W Z

A

A record

A DNS record that points a hostname to an IPv4 address, such as 192.0.2.10.

AAAA record

A DNS record that points a hostname to an IPv6 address.

ads.txt

A public text file at the root of a website that lists the companies authorized to sell its ad space, used to fight ad fraud.

Aftermarket

The resale market for domain names that are already registered, through marketplaces, auctions, brokers or direct deals.

Auth code

A secret code (also called EPP code or transfer key) that the current registrar issues to authorize moving a domain to another registrar.

Auto-renew grace period

A window after expiry, commonly up to 45 days for .com, during which the registrant can usually still renew, often at the normal price.

B

Backorder

A request placed with a drop-catching service to try to register a domain the moment it is deleted.

BIMI

Brand Indicators for Message Identification: a DNS record that lets supporting inboxes show a verified logo next to authenticated email.

Brandable domain

A name chosen for being short, distinctive and easy to say rather than for exact keywords, such as an invented or blended word.

Broker

A professional who negotiates a domain purchase or sale for a client, usually for a success fee or commission.

C

CAA record

Certification Authority Authorization: a DNS record that names which certificate authorities may issue TLS certificates for a domain.

ccTLD

Country-code top-level domain: a two-letter extension assigned to a country or territory, such as .ca, .in or .uk.

Certificate transparency

Public, append-only logs of every TLS certificate issued, which let owners spot certificates created for look-alike domains.

clientTransferProhibited

A status code set by the registrar that blocks transfers to another registrar until the owner removes the lock.

CNAME record

A DNS record that makes one hostname an alias of another, such as www pointing to the main site's hostname.

Comparable sales

Reported sale prices of similar domains, used as the main evidence when estimating what a name is worth.

Cybersquatting

Registering a domain that matches someone else's trademark in bad faith, usually to profit from confusion or resale.

D

DKIM

DomainKeys Identified Mail: a signature added to outgoing email, verified with a public key published in DNS.

DMARC

A DNS policy that tells receiving mail servers what to do with messages that fail SPF and DKIM checks, and where to send reports.

DNS

The Domain Name System: the internet's directory that turns names people type into the addresses computers use.

DNSSEC

DNS Security Extensions: cryptographic signatures on DNS answers that let resolvers detect forged or tampered records.

Domain lease

An agreement that lets someone use a domain for a recurring fee, sometimes with an option to buy it outright.

Drop

The moment a deleted domain is released by the registry and becomes available to register again.

DS record

Delegation Signer: a record held at the parent zone that links a domain's DNSSEC keys to the chain of trust.

E

EPP status codes

Standard codes in a registry record, such as ok, clientHold or pendingDelete, that describe what can and cannot happen to a domain.

Escrow

A neutral third party that holds the buyer's payment until the seller has transferred the domain, then releases the funds.

Exact-match domain

A domain that repeats a search phrase word for word, such as bestcoffeegrinders.com.

Expiration date

The date in the registry record when a registration ends unless it is renewed.

G

gTLD

Generic top-level domain: an extension not tied to a country, such as .com, .org or the newer .app and .works.

H

Hand registration

Registering an available domain at the standard registrar price instead of buying it from someone else.

Homograph attack

Using characters that look alike, such as the Cyrillic а and the Latin a, to make a fake domain look like a real one.

HSTS

HTTP Strict Transport Security: a header that tells browsers to only connect to a site over HTTPS.

I

IANA

The Internet Assigned Numbers Authority, which maintains the root zone that lists every top-level domain.

ICANN

The Internet Corporation for Assigned Names and Numbers, the non-profit that coordinates domain name policy and accredits registrars.

IDN

Internationalized domain name: a domain using non-ASCII characters, stored in DNS as an xn-- form called Punycode.

Inbound inquiry

An offer or question from a would-be buyer who found your domain, as opposed to one you went out and pitched.

L

Lander

A simple page shown on a domain that is for sale, usually with a price or an offer form.

Lookalike domain

A domain built to resemble another, through typos, swapped letters, extra words or a different extension.

M

MTA-STS

A policy published over HTTPS and announced in DNS that tells mail servers to require encrypted, verified connections when delivering to you.

MX record

Mail exchange record: tells other servers which hosts accept email for a domain, with a priority number for each.

N

Nameserver

A server that holds a domain's DNS records and answers questions about them. The registry lists which nameservers are authoritative.

NS record

A DNS record that names the authoritative nameservers for a domain or zone.

P

Parking

Pointing an unused domain at a page of ads or a for-sale notice instead of a real website.

Pending delete

The final stage, usually five days, before a registry deletes an expired domain. It cannot be renewed during this period.

Premium domain

A name priced above the standard rate, either by its owner on the aftermarket or by the registry itself.

Propagation

The time it takes for DNS changes to be seen everywhere, governed by the TTL of the old records.

Punycode

The encoding that turns international characters into ASCII for DNS, producing names that start with xn--.

R

RDAP

Registration Data Access Protocol: the modern, structured replacement for WHOIS that returns registration data as JSON.

Redemption grace period

A period after a domain is deleted by the registrar, typically 30 days for .com, when the last owner can still restore it for a higher fee.

Registrant

The person or organization that holds the right to use a domain name.

Registrar

A company accredited to sell domain registrations and manage them in the registry on behalf of customers.

Registry

The organization that runs a top-level domain's database and nameservers, such as Verisign for .com.

Registry lock

A high-security status that blocks changes to a domain until the registry verifies the request manually.

Renewal price

What a registrar charges to extend a registration after the first year, often higher than the introductory price.

Reverse WHOIS

A search for every domain registered with the same name, organization or email address.

Root zone

The top of the DNS hierarchy, listing every top-level domain and the nameservers responsible for it.

S

SLD

Second-level domain: the part directly left of the extension, such as example in example.com.

SOA record

Start of Authority: a DNS record holding a zone's primary nameserver, contact and timing values.

SPF

Sender Policy Framework: a TXT record listing which servers may send email for a domain.

Subdomain

A name under a domain, such as shop.example.com, managed by whoever controls the parent domain's DNS.

T

TLD

Top-level domain: the last label of a domain name, such as .com, .ca or .works.

Transfer lock

A period, often 60 days after registration or an ownership change, during which a domain cannot move to another registrar.

TTL

Time to live: how many seconds resolvers may cache a DNS answer before asking again.

TXT record

A DNS record holding free text, used for SPF, DMARC, domain verification and other policies.

Typosquatting

Registering misspellings of popular domains to capture mistyped traffic, often for ads or phishing.

U

UDRP

Uniform Domain-Name Dispute-Resolution Policy: the ICANN process for trademark owners to recover domains registered in bad faith.

Uptime monitoring

Automated checks that a website responds, with alerts when it stops.

W

WHOIS

The original protocol for looking up domain registration data, now largely replaced by RDAP for generic TLDs.

WHOIS privacy

A service that hides the registrant's personal contact details in public lookups, often included free by registrars.

Z

Zone file

The list of DNS records for a domain or, at a registry, the list of every registered name with its nameservers.
